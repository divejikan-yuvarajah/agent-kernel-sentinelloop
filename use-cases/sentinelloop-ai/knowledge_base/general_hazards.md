# General Workplace Hazards

## Immediate worker actions
- Keep a safe distance from the hazard.
- Warn nearby workers about the dangerous condition.
- Stop using unsafe equipment if this can be done safely.
- Do not enter an area that looks unstable or unsafe.
- Keep people away from spills, damaged equipment, or blocked paths.
- Report the hazard immediately through the site safety process.
- Wait until the area or equipment has been confirmed safe before returning.

## Site regulations
- Every worker has the right and duty to report unsafe conditions without delay.
- Walkways, emergency routes, and loading bays must remain clear of obstructions.
- Spills, leaks, and damaged flooring must be marked and reported until cleaned.
- Defective tools and equipment must be tagged out and not returned to service.
- Housekeeping is part of safety: waste, offcuts, and pallets must be stacked safely.
- Visitors must be escorted and must follow the same access and PPE rules.
- Night and shift handovers must mention open hazards still waiting for control.
- Photographs and reports used for incidents must not include unnecessary personal data.
- Do not disable guards, alarms, or safety signs to save time.
- High-risk work needs a supervisor or permit where the site procedure requires it.

## Worker duties
- Look for slip, trip, fall, heat, noise, and housekeeping hazards at the start of a shift.
- Use the correct tool for the job and inspect it before use.
- Keep a safe distance from forklifts, overhead loads, and reversing vehicles.
- Speak up if a coworker is in immediate danger and call for help if needed.
- Follow posted speed, access, and exclusion-zone signs.

## Prohibited actions
- Do not invent repair steps or improvise unsafe shortcuts.
- Do not ignore a hazard because production is busy.
- Do not remove warning tape, tags, or barriers placed by the safety team.
- Do not enter confined, unstable, or poorly lit spaces without authorization.

Always follow instructions from trained safety or emergency personnel.
